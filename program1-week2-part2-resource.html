Inclusive Audit Tool

Week 2 · Accessibility, Disability Justice, and Emergent Learning

This tool walks you through each of the three pillars — what it is, what it asks of your environment, and how to observe it in practice — before you begin your audit. Record your findings as you go. A summary you can print or save generates automatically.

Mode
Pillar 1 · Infrastructure
Accessibility
The legal and technical floor that makes participation possible
What it is

Accessibility is the removal of physical, technological, and attitudinal barriers to learning. It is grounded in legal frameworks — the Accessible Canada Act, the ADA, the AODA — and technical standards like WCAG 2.1 (perceivable, operable, understandable, robust). It is the floor, not the ceiling: compliance establishes minimum conditions for participation, not equity of experience.

“Does this policy treat legal compliance as a starting point — or as the destination?”
What to look for

Retrieve your institution’s accessibility policy or a recent compliance report. Is the language reactive (“accommodations provided on request”) or proactive (“designed for access from the outset”)? Note whether it names specific standards or stays general.

Observation prompt
Locate one piece of content in your environment. Apply the WCAG lens: perceivable, operable, understandable, robust. Where does it pass? Where does it fail — and for whom?
Team prompt
Each person audits one piece of content independently, then compare. Where do your observations converge — and where do they differ? What does the difference reveal about whose experience of the system is most visible to your team?

Pillar 2 · Ethics
Disability justice
The compass that asks who leads, who benefits, and who is still missing
What it is

Disability justice moves beyond the legal floor to a political question: who was in the room when this system was designed, and whose needs were written in as default? It centres intersectionality — a framework developed by legal scholar Kimberlé Crenshaw to describe how race, gender, class, disability, and other axes of identity compound one another, producing forms of exclusion that single-axis frameworks cannot see or address. A core principle is the leadership of those most impacted: inclusion designed without input from those excluded tends to replicate the conditions that created the gap.

“Do the people leading this program reflect the communities most affected by its gaps?”
What to look for

Look at who holds decision-making authority in the program you are auditing. Then look at who it serves. Apply an intersectional lens: are these the same people? Where are the power gaps? You are looking for evidence of whose perspective shapes the design.

Observation prompt
Name one historically, persistently, or systemically marginalized (HPSM) group of learners in your current environment. What would need to change if their experience were the design brief — not an edge case?
Team prompt
Each person names one HPSM group in your environment. Map where they overlap. Which exclusions were visible to everyone — and which required someone to name them?
Your observations
Pillar 3 · Pedagogy
Emergent learning
The method that keeps the system responsive
What it is

Emergent learning treats curriculum as a living system rather than a fixed delivery mechanism. Rooted in Freire’s critique of the “banking model” — where learners are passive recipients — it creates conditions for real-time adaptation, peer-based inquiry, and context-driven response. Adaptation is not a sign of failure; it is skilled practice. Systems that can adapt are more resilient than those that cannot.

“Can you find one moment where someone responded to what was actually occurring — rather than following a fixed plan?”
What to look for

Observe a live or recorded session, or review a collaborative workspace. Look for genuine response: a facilitator who changed direction based on learner feedback, a peer reframe that shifted the group. If you cannot find one, that absence is a data point.

Observation prompt
Identify one moment where your environment responded to learners rather than delivered to them. What made that possible — and what would need to change for it to be the norm?
Team prompt
Each person shares one example of emergence they observed. Then ask collectively: were those conditions deliberate or accidental — and how do you build more of them?
Your observations
Map the silos
Where the circles overlap, your system is integrating the pillars. Where they do not, the gap is your research territory.
After completing all three pillars, consider where your environment integrates these elements and where they operate as isolated silos. The gaps are not failures — they are the starting point for your inquiry.
