Thesis supervision

Meetings · feedback · guidance

Assumed participant
Available 9–5 on weekdays, fluent in email, able to attend synchronous in-person or video meetings, with a stable schedule that doesn't shift week to week.
Gap identified
No asynchronous communication option built into the supervision model. Overnight and shift-based availability is not accommodated by default. Gaps in supervision can accumulate across weeks without a formal mechanism to flag them.
Reframe written ✓
"Student missed the meeting" → "The supervision system assumed 9–5 weekday availability. The student's overnight care schedule made that window consistently inaccessible." The failure is a design failure, not an attendance failure.
Peer question
Where is ambiguity built into supervision expectations — and what does that cost neurodivergent students specifically? Are expectations about feedback timelines, revision rounds, and communication norms written down anywhere, or are they assumed?

Course management system

LMS · content · assessment

Assumed participant
Has reliable desktop or laptop access, consistent institutional wifi, no assistive technology requirements, and the capacity to engage with time-locked or session-bound content formats.
Gap identified
Content is not consistently available offline. Screen reader compatibility has not been tested across all module types. Video content lacks reliable captions. Assignment submission interfaces vary in accessibility across course sections.
Still to investigate
Are multiple assessment formats available, or is written text the default across all courses? Who can request alternatives, and through what process?

Library and research access

Access · resources · stacks

Assumed participant
Can access the physical campus consistently, maintains an uninterrupted institutional login, and can navigate print-first collections without physical barriers. Assumes standard working-hours access to librarian support.
Gap identified
Physical access to stacks is not consistently barrier-free. Interlibrary loan timelines assume a researcher with flexible scheduling. After-hours and remote access to specialist support is limited or unavailable.
Still to investigate
What is the financial cost of required research materials beyond institutional access? Are there hidden monetary barriers — required textbooks, specific software, or travel to collect materials — that are not acknowledged in programme costs?

Disability services

Accommodations · support

Assumed participant
Student initiates contact, self-discloses, re-registers each term, and follows up with individual instructors to ensure accommodations are actioned. The system assumes the administrative labour falls on the student.
Gap identified
Accommodation letters are issued to instructors but are not consistently actioned. There is no formal mechanism for the student to verify that accommodations are in place without additional follow-up. The burden of ensuring compliance rests with the person least positioned to enforce it.
Peer question
Are student perspectives actually centred in how accommodations are designed — or do well-intentioned interventions still happen to students rather than with them? And when a gap in the system causes harm, who is accountable? Does the institution take responsibility for accommodation failures, or is the student expected to manage the gap?
Still to investigate
What happens when the same gap recurs? Is there a mechanism for pattern recognition across student complaints, or does each case remain isolated?

Scheduling and assessment

Exams · rubrics · feedback timing

Assumed participant
Can sit exams during standard scheduled windows, brings shared cultural knowledge to course texts and examples, and has access to the same real-time information as all other students during assessments.
Gap identified
Students sitting exams in alternate locations or times may not receive the same mid-exam information — corrections, clarifications, typo notices — as students in the primary room. Cultural knowledge assumed by texts and rubrics is not acknowledged as an assumption; gaps are read as student deficits.
Peer question
What prior cultural knowledge is embedded in the learning itself — in the texts selected, the examples used, the references assumed to be shared? A gap in cultural background is frequently misread as a deficit in reading ability or intelligence rather than as a gap in representation. Does the material reflect a range of cultural contexts? Are student evaluations collected mid-course and used to adjust current teaching — or only gathered at the end, when it is too late to change anything for the students who completed it?
Still to investigate
Is there a mechanism to ensure real-time exam communications reach all students equally, regardless of where they are sitting? How is this currently managed, and who is responsible when it fails?
